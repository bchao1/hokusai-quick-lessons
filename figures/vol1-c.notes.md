# Volume 1, PDF pages 18–23: notes for the rulebook

## p.18: fish and a sparrow
- A fish on bamboo leaves (left): the body is two long shallow arcs (back and belly), the head a ruled forehead line with a small eye circle and a bracket for the gill. The leaves under it are plain ruled lines (an X plus horizontals). In the finished print the body is solid dark with vertical hatching and one pale band along the side.
- The large curved sea bream (right) is a crescent between two big compass arcs. The forked tail is two more crescents, a circle sitting on the back line gives the dorsal fin, and the head is a lozenge with two concentric eye circles. The finished fish is shown in a different, mirrored pose over the construction, covered in rows of arc scales.
- Sparrow (bottom): each raised wing is a thin crescent, and a small lozenge marks the shoulder where the wings join. The head is a small circle with a wedge beak. The finished bird is a flurry of short dark feather strokes, not outlines.

## p.19: blowfish, turban shell, octopus
- Blowfish: one large circle for the belly and a long arc over it for the back, which runs into two ruled lines for the tail. The eye is two concentric circles and the mouth a circle with a cross. Finished: a dark mottled back over a pale, stippled belly, with large dots along the flank.
- Turban shell: a small circle (apex) inside a medium circle inside a large one, and a triangle pulled out to the tip of the shell.
- Octopus: the mantle is a lens made by two arcs, the head is a circle with a concentric eye, and the arms are stacked arcs. Finished: the whole skin is stippled and the arms carry sucker marks.

## p.20: butterfly dancer, bat and moon
- Butterfly dancer (left): the four wings are four large overlapping circles, the head is a small circle, and the sleeves are lozenges. A ruled screen fills the background.
- Bat: the drawing is mirror-symmetric. Each wing is a long leading-edge arc and a wide concave trailing arc, with small circles biting scallops at the thumb and the hip. Ears and face are small circles and the legs a half circle. One large circle behind it is the moon.
- The finished bat shows the tone rule: the sky is flat dark ink with paler cloud bands, the moon is left as bare paper, and the bat is mid-grey with a pale furry body.

## p.21: lion dancer, courtier with parasol
- Lion dancer: two large circles joined by ruled tangent lines make the cloth hanging over the dancer. Three small circles in a row on a ruled box make the mask, one ruled line runs from the jaw to the centre of the lower circle, and a zigzag of straight lines makes the walking legs. The fence behind is only ruled verticals and horizontals.
- Courtier (right): made only with the ruler. A fan-shaped arc band is the parasol, a lozenge with one black quarter is the head and hat, and big tilted parallelograms are the robe. The finished version keeps those flat planes and adds fan crests and a black lacquered hat.

## p.22: crouching boy, standing lantern, scroll
- Crouching boy: a cluster of four circles makes the head and back, three circles the knees and seat, a lozenge the crest on the back, and one long ruled line across everything is the sword. Finished: the robe is flat black with the lozenge left white.
- Standing lantern: a cylinder, meaning two parallel verticals between a flattened lens top and bottom. Finished: a dark top and base, a wavy paper edge, and horizontal bamboo hatching.
- Two women with a hanging scroll (right): each woman's head and body are clusters of circles. The pine clumps above are also circle clusters, which become radiating needle bursts in the finished print.

## p.23: fisherman with net, paper lantern, woman in sedge hat
- Fisherman: a large circle for the sedge hat, overlapping circles behind it for the straw raincoat, a ruled triangle for the scoop net, and a ruled pole. Water is nested crescent arcs, and hanging strands above are vertical ruled lines. Finished: the hat gets radial ribs and a ring of dots, the net is cross-hatched, and the raincoat is dense hair strokes (kebiki).
- Paper lantern: two equal circles overlap vertically and their outer edge is the lantern. Small ruled boxes make the cap and base, and the finished ribs are straight horizontal lines.
- Woman in a sedge hat: the hat is an ellipse with a small circle at each side. The kimono is three stacked tilted squares whose corners make the elbow, the hip and the kicked-out hem, closed by one compass arc. Finished: chrysanthemum crests (radiating bursts) and a striped dark obi.
