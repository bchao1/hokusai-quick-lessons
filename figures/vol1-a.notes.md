# Vol. 1, PDF pages 5–11: notes for the rulebook

- **p.5:** Two shishi, each shown next to its construction. The top lion is one big circle holding a smaller face circle, with a crown of circles on top for the mane and two long arcs on the right for the tail. The seated lion below is a stack of about a dozen overlapping circles. In the finished lions every mane or cloud circle is inked as a tight spiral, and the body is covered in short curved hair strokes.
- **p.6:** Fukurokuju is a vertical stack: a small crown circle, two nearly parallel ruled lines for the tall skull, a face circle cut by spokes (the lines of brows and nose), a large body circle with a ruled V collar, a ruled box for the skirt, and two circles for the feet. The child on his head and the fan at his feet are small circles too. The octopus is one head circle and two eye circles, with a ring of arm circles that become spirals. Daruma (same spread) is rounded boxes.
- **p.7:** Ruler shapes set the costume. The kamishimo shoulder wings are one wide lozenge, the hakama is a fan swung from its top point, and a big circle behind them gives the black sleeves. The traveller's sedge hat is a tall triangle, and her cloak is a black triangle. The umbrella is a circle with a lozenge and a small circle at the hub, four ruled spokes, radial ribs and a circle crest. Its bearer is a triangle for the hat, a circle for the head, a lozenge for the shoulders and a circle for the knee. Rain is bands of ruled diagonals behind everything.
- **p.8:** Animals start from circles, or from a square with a lozenge laid over it. For the sitting dog, the square with its overhanging corners inked black gives the head and ear patches, and circles give chest, haunch and tail. The puppy is overlapping circles for head, ears and muzzle. Vine leaves are hexagons split into six triangles, with the veins drawn along those lines. Grapes are small circles, and the squirrel's tail is a large S made of arcs.
- **p.9:** The lying ox is a ruled wedge for the body, with circles for chest, belly and rump. Its horns are two spans of one compass circle, and the head is a triangle with a smaller black triangle as the forelock. Its neck folds are stepped arcs. The horse seen from behind is two overlapping equal circles for the rump, with the cleft inked where they cross, plus arcs for the neck. The rat is a cluster of circles with a small square for the muzzle.
- **p.10:** The crane is a head circle with a wedge beak, a ruled trapezoid for the body and one long arc for the wing. The legs are two ruled lines, and the toes run along the edges of two lozenges. Hotei is a column of circles (head, jowls, two chest circles, a great belly) set in front of a tall rounded box for his sack. The flying bird is built inside a square: a big circle arc divides it, and black triangles in its corners become the wings.
- **p.11:** Insects are fitted into triangles and trapezoids. On the butterfly, the black corner triangles are left over once the four wing circles are inside the frame. The dragonfly, mantis and grasshopper are built from long ruled lines and narrow triangles. The hanging monkey is a head circle with two small ear circles and a body circle below. The arm is two long ruled lines up to the branch, and the finished monkey is all black fur.
