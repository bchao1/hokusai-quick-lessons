# Volume 1, pp. 24–29 — notes

## p.24
- Two figures, each shown built from shapes and then finished. Monkey behind a sign: a tall triangle crossed by an inverted one gives the cap and shoulders; the face circle holds two overlapping eye circles and a muzzle circle; the sign is a ruled rectangle split by one diagonal, with one circle per panel (the peaches).
- Man carrying a drum, in straw coat and sedge hat: the hat is a ruled four-sided figure with a curved lower edge, the body is one large circle, the drum is three concentric circles, and the legs are two lozenges already filled solid black in the construction. The solid black survives unchanged as the black leggings.
- Bamboo grass starts as small ruled "roof" shapes with dashed vertical stems.

## p.25
- Seated courtier by the sea: a stack of circles (head, torso, lap, two circles for the robe trailing to the right) inside one ruled rectangle for the back, and a black-filled triangle for the cap. Behind him, a band of ruled lozenge lattice in perspective; in the finished print the lattice becomes rows of small peaked wave marks.
- Child pushing a huge snowball, with a centipede: the snowball is one big circle with a chain of small circles along its edge, and the finished edge is scalloped.

## p.26
- Three masks, each a cluster of circles with the features placed on the circle centres and where the circles meet. Demon: six circles in a ring round a solid nose circle, plus two tall thin triangles for horns. The jaw edge is inked as bristles. Hyottoko: one eye is two concentric circles and the pursed mouth is a small ellipse. Okame: two brow circles hung from one ruled line, with wider cheek circles and a chin circle.
- Traveller in rain: the hat is two nested circles (the finished hat is a dark ring cut by radial ribs), the straw coat is two big overlapping circles, the staff is one ruled line, and the rain is a field of steep ruled diagonals across the whole panel.
- A small bird in flight is built from a circle, a flat chevron and crossing lines.

## p.27
- Boar: the whole body is one tilted lozenge (snout, hump, rump, belly at its corners). Compass arcs at each end mark the fore and hind leg joins, and the legs are bent ruled lines. The finished contour is broken bristle strokes that stay inside the lozenge.
- Three flowers on one vertical axis, each with a construction column next to it. Morning glory: five circles for the face, a triangle for the tube, circles cut by ruled lines for the leaf lobes. Iris: a circle holding a lens for the upright petals, lens shapes for the side petals, a lower circle with an inscribed triangle, and a kite for the sheath. Maple: a regular hexagon with the lobe tips at its corners and straight veins from the centre.
- In the finished iris the petals are flat solid black (nuri) with white stripes left in them. The morning glory and maple are outline only.

## p.28
- Two arched bridges: the arch is a compass arc and the deck is ruled parallels. One bridge has arched openings, and banks of rounded bushes are drawn as rows of half-circles.
- Two figures in lion masks built from circles and rectangles, finished with solid black robes.
- Resting deer: a head circle with triangle ears on a large chest circle, one wide ellipse for the folded body with a haunch circle inside, and a triangle for the tucked foreleg. Antlers are ruled branching lines. The maple leaves start as six-ray asterisks and are finished as small solid leaves.

## p.29
- Waves: rows of overlapping long compass arcs. The distant rows are left as bare lines; the near rows are filled with parallel strokes that follow each arc.
- A plover in flight, drawn small against the waves, with a separate little construction of crossing ruled lines and triangles beside it.
