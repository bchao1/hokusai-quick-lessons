# Volume 1, pp. 12–17: notes for the rulebook

- **p.12:** The packhorse is three circles in a row for chest, barrel and rump. A ruled wedge makes the neck, and small hexagons make the lowered head. Each leg is a ruled tube with a circle at the knee and at the fetlock and a triangle for the hoof. The rider is a slanted parallelogram-and-triangle hat over three small circles, with one long compass arc for the back of the cape. In the finished horse the lower legs, ears and tail are solid black, and the coat is short hair strokes.
- **p.12:** The pine is a double-ruled zigzag branch hung with clusters of equal circles. One circle is drawn with spokes to show that its needles radiate from the centre. When finished, each circle keeps only its upper arc, and its lower half becomes a fan of needles.
- **p.13:** The rooster and hen are built from squares tilted about 40°. Each has one square for the head, one for the neck and a row of squares for the body. The hen's body and tail are each a square twice the size of the head square. Areas that will be solid black (hackle, breast, the tail triangle below or above a diagonal) are already blacked in on the construction.
- **p.13:** The rabbit leaping over waves is built from small circles for the rabbit and stacked scalloped arcs with curled ends for the crests, with loose circles for the spray. Not transcribed.
- **p.14:** Birds in flight use lozenges for the wings, with a star of ruled lines radiating from the body's centre. The wing tips are blacked-in triangles, and the head is a small lozenge with an eye dot. Geese on the ground are parallelograms ruled into grids: a narrow one for head and neck, and a three-by-two grid for the body. The finished feather rows follow the grid rows.
- **p.15:** Small birds are ovals and circles: a head circle, a long body arc or egg shape, two overlapping circles for the folded wing, an oval for the thigh and a ruled strip for the tail. Seen from the front, the body is an egg with a smaller egg inside it for the face and a circle below for the feet. The mandarin ducks on water are stacked circles with nested arcs for the wing feathers (not transcribed).
- **p.16:** The court noble seen from behind is a lozenge with a small square for the cap, circles for head and shoulders, and a circle on a trapezoid for the black centre of the robe. The sleeves are two ruled wedges filled with lattice. The straw-caped figure beside him and the horse coming out of a gourd use a semicircle, vertical rulings, a stack of circles and a chain of alternating arcs (not transcribed).
- **p.17:** The catfish is two long compass sweeps that meet at the tail, with a pile of small circles for the blunt head, ringed circles for the eyes, and two-arc lenses for the fins. The water weed is strings of small circles, and each circle is inked as a starburst of needles. The body is a flat grey-black mass.
